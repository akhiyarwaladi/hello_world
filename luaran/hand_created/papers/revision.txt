Reviewer's recommendation:

1. The introduction should contextualize your research and provide any specialized information that the general measurement or control reader may require to comprehend what follows. It must explain the significance of previous work as well as the issues that your work addresses. It should also contain a list of comparable works. The introduction should define the article's contribution(s) and show how it is demonstrated throughout the manuscript. A typical introduction should be as oncise as possible and include the following:
a). An outline of the problem.
b). A review of the relevant literature, highlighting briefly the major
contributors and indicating:
- What the main contributors did?
- What the main contributors found?

2. The method section must provide a detailed step-by-step description of the experimental procedure, including all of the information required to replicate the work described in the paper. The Method must include a description of both novel and standard experimental approaches, as well as any necessary justification to persuade the reader that the methods are correct.

3. The discussion informs readers about your study's larger implications based on the findings. Highlighting these implications without overstating the findings can be difficult, especially if you're submitting to a journal that chooses articles based on novelty or potential impact. Regardless of which journal you submit to, the discussion section always serves the same purpose: to summarize what your study's findings mean. A successful discussion section puts your findings in context. It should include:
- the results of your research,
- a discussion of related research, and
- a comparison between your results and initial hypothesis.

4. Your conclusion is more than just a summary of what you've previously written. It should enhance your paper by addressing any outstanding questions. Summarize what you've discovered in your research and propose potential applications and extensions. The primary question in your conclusion should be, "What do my findings mean for the research field and my community?"
